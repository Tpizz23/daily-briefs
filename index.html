Sermon Review

Governing Authorities and the Law of Love — Day 5 of 6

Lee McDerment | October 4, 2026 | Romans 13

Summary: Lee McDerment addresses the tension Christians feel between submitting to governing authorities and remaining faithful to God. Drawing from Romans 13, the sermon frames civic responsibility as part of Christian discipleship while keeping love for others as the governing ethic: love fulfills the law.

Key takeaways: Christians are called to live as responsible citizens; submission to government is not blind allegiance because loyalty to God remains ultimate; and the practical center of Romans 13 is love expressed through humility, integrity, and active concern for neighbors.

Reflection: Romans 13 calls for a posture that is both principled and peaceable—respecting authority while remembering that every authority is accountable to God. The clearest test is whether a response protects truth and pursues the good of others.

Today’s application: Consider where political identity competes with loyalty to God, then let love for neighbors shape how you speak about authorities and people who disagree.
