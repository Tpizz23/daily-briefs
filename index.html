Sermon Review

"Sermon: What Will You Leave Behind?"

Series: Made for Relationships · Preacher: Dan Lian

TODAY'S FOCUS: The Freedom of Leaving Something That Lasts

KEY SCRIPTURE

"Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal. But store up for yourselves treasures in heaven." — Matthew 6:19–20

INSIGHT

One of the most freeing implications of Dan's sermon was the promise that relational investment is the one kind of legacy that actually lasts — and that outlasts every other kind of storage. Matthew 6:19–20 is often applied to financial generosity, but its deeper logic applies here: moth and rust (deterioration) and thieves (unforeseen loss) target what we build for ourselves; what we invest in people stores up treasures that no external force can destroy. The sermon offered a reframe: instead of asking "What will I leave behind?" as a burden or an obligation, we can ask it as an invitation. There is profound freedom in knowing that your most meaningful contribution to the world might be a single faithful relationship, a hard conversation you had the courage to initiate, or a prayer you prayed when no one was watching. These are treasures in heaven — they compound across generations in ways we will only fully see in eternity.

TODAY'S APPLICATION

Identify one treasure you have been holding onto — a grudge, a fear, a preference for comfort over vulnerability — and ask God to exchange it for the freedom of generativity. Write down what relational investment you could make this week if you truly believed it would outlast everything else you have worked for.

CLOSING PRAYER

Father, You promise that what is laid up in heaven does not decay or get stolen. Help me to stop investing so heavily in the things that do. Free me from the fear of being used up or worn out by relational investment — because You promise that those who water others get watered themselves (1 Corinthians 3:6–8). Give me the joy of storing up treasure in heaven through the people I love and serve. Amen.
