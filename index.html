Sermon Review

“When Screens Take Over” · Made For Relationships · Joey Odom

TODAY'S FOCUS: Sustaining a Covenant of Presence

KEY SCRIPTURE

Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 — “Two are better than one because they have a good return for their labor. If either should fall, the one will lift up his companion. But how will it be with the one who falls and does not have another to lift him up?”

INSIGHT

The Book of Ecclesiastes acknowledges what technology cannot replace: the mutual strengthening that happens when two or more people are fully present with each other. Odom's story was ultimately one of covenant — a husband and father choosing, again and again, to show up for his family rather than default to the convenience of his phone. Sustaining these changes requires more than willpower; it requires community. We need friends who will ask us the hard questions, spouses who will call us back when we drift, and children who will demand our attention in ways that are holy and inconvenient. The goal of a week of reflection on this sermon is not to feel conviction once but to build a durable habit of presence that strengthens over time.

TODAY'S APPLICATION

This weekend, have an honest conversation with someone close to you about what you learned this week. Ask them: “Do you feel I am fully present with you when we are together, or do I often default to my phone?” Listen with humility and receive their answer without becoming defensive. Together, identify one practical step you can both commit to for the sake of your relationship.

CLOSING PRAYER

God, You designed me for community, not isolation. Thank You for the people You have placed in my life who know me, call me higher, and love me even when I am distracted and disengaged. Forgive me for the times I have been physically present but spiritually absent. Teach me to receive accountability with grace and to offer it with kindness. As I enter another week, I want to be a person whose presence is a gift — steady, attentive, and full of the life You have given me. Help me to sustain this journey with Your strength, not my own. Amen.
