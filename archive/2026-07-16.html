Sermon Review

When Screens Take Over — Day 4: Thursday

Theme: Building Technology Rhythms That Protect Your Relationships

Key Scripture: Colossians 3:23 — “Whatever you do, do it from the soul, as something done for the Lord and not for men.”

Sermon Insight: Odom was vulnerable in sharing his household's struggle with screen overwhelm, and his solution was not to go analog entirely but to be intentional about when, where, and how technology was used. Small changes in household rhythms — like phone charging stations outside the bedroom or shared device-free meals — rewire the default. Colossians 3 reminds us that everything done as unto the Lord carries eternal weight, including ordinary moments that feel unremarkable. Choosing to put the phone down during dinner, or not checking it while someone wants to talk, is an act of worship. These disciplines are not about legalism; they are about creating space for love to do its work.

Today’s Application: Implement one new technology boundary this week with the people you live with. Ideas: no phones at the dinner table, no devices in the bedroom after 9pm, phone on airplane mode during conversations, or a weekly screen-free afternoon. Announce the boundary to those around you so they can hold you accountable. Observe how it changes the quality of conversations and attention.

Closing Prayer: Lord, I want the ordinary moments of my daily life to be an offering to You. Give me creativity and persistence to build new rhythms that protect my attention for the people You have given me to love. When I fail at the boundaries I set, remind me that Your grace is greater than my weakness. Help me not to wallow in guilt but to get back up and try again. Let those around me see a person who chooses presence over distraction, not perfectly, but persistently. Amen.
